What it is

Scroll Scrape turns places you save from Instagram into plans. You save a place from a screenshot or caption, browse your saves by city on a world map, ask the flamingo to plan a day using only your saves, and file outing photos under the places you visited (Memories).

One action, end to end: “plan a day”

  1. Browser app/(app)/plan/PlanChat.tsx, search for async function send.
    Sends the whole conversation plus today's day and time to fetch("/api/plan"). Sending the history is what lets “yes, plan the rest of the day” build on the earlier plan.
  2. Server app/api/plan/route.ts, search for export async function POST.
    Matches the city against your own spaces, loads only your places from Supabase, and asks Gemini (askJson<PlanResult>) to answer with place IDs. Then it checks opening hours (lib/hours.ts) and asks Google for transit times (travelMinutes( in lib/maps.ts).
  3. Server lib/guard.ts, search for keepSavedStops.
    Drops any ID that is not one of your saved places, and any repeats, before the plan reaches you. node scripts/check-guard.ts feeds it a made-up ID to confirm.

A practice to reuse

Enforce the product's promise in code, then prove it with a small check. “Plans come only from your saves” is Scroll Scrape's core rule (scope.md › The Unique Kernel). Asking Gemini nicely was not enough, so the plan route makes Gemini answer with IDs. keepSavedStops then throws away anything else, and scripts/check-guard.ts proves it with a made-up ID.

The same habit caught a real bug in Memories. A test photo with a known location came back “no location”, which exposed that reading only some fields skipped the step that computes latitude and longitude. On your next project, write down the one rule your app must never break. Put it in a function you can test on its own, and check it with a real input.
